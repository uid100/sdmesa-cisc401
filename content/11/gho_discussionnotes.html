Instructor Discussion Notes: Exploring the WHO Global Health Observatory (GHO) API

Discussion Goal

This activity introduces students to a real-world public dataset and provides an opportunity to explore how databases are used to store, retrieve, filter, and analyze information. Students will investigate data available through the WHO Global Health Observatory (GHO) API and share their findings with the class.

Introduction

Ask students:

How do governments and health organizations know whether global health is improving or getting worse?

Guide students toward the idea that decisions are often based on data collected from many sources and stored in large databases. The WHO GHO API provides access to this information.

Connecting the Activity to Database Concepts

As students explore the API, encourage them to identify examples of concepts discussed in class:

  • Data collection
  • Data storage
  • Data retrieval
  • Queries and filters
  • Data analysis
  • Data sharing through APIs

Ask:

Why would an organization provide an API instead of asking users to download entire datasets manually?

Possible responses include faster access, current information, smaller data transfers, and more flexible queries.

Exploring the Dataset

Students may choose any dataset or health indicator that interests them. Possible topics include:

  • Life expectancy
  • Vaccination rates
  • Child health
  • Air pollution
  • Tuberculosis
  • Malaria
  • Maternal health
  • Nutrition
  • Health workforce statistics
  • Environmental health

Ask students:

What health issue interests you most, and why?

Discussion Prompts

As students prepare their initial posts, encourage them to think beyond simply reporting data.

Questions to guide analysis:

  • What indicator did you choose?
  • Why did you select that topic?
  • What query or filters did you use?
  • What patterns or trends did you observe?
  • Did any results surprise you?
  • Did you notice differences between countries or regions?
  • What additional questions does the data raise?

Understanding Queries and Filters

Remind students that APIs allow users to retrieve specific information rather than all available data.

Ask:

How does filtering data make analysis easier?

Guide the discussion toward concepts such as:

  • Reducing the amount of data returned
  • Focusing on a specific country or region
  • Comparing different time periods
  • Answering targeted research questions

Data Quality and Interpretation

Ask students:

Can numbers alone tell the entire story behind a health issue?

Discuss how data may vary because of:

  • Reporting methods
  • Data availability
  • Economic conditions
  • Healthcare access
  • Government policies
  • Cultural and regional differences

Remind students that interpreting data requires both technical and critical thinking skills.

Responding to Classmates

Encourage students to move beyond simple agreement when responding to peers.

Suggested response ideas:

  • Ask follow-up questions about the results.
  • Compare findings with a different region or country.
  • Suggest an additional query or filter.
  • Discuss possible explanations for observed trends.
  • Connect the findings to another health issue.

Critical Thinking Questions

  1. What types of health data appear to be most useful for policymakers?
  2. What challenges might exist when collecting data from many countries?
  3. How might incomplete or inaccurate data affect decision-making?
  4. What advantages does an API provide over static reports?
  5. How can databases and APIs help address global health challenges?

Key Takeaway

The WHO Global Health Observatory demonstrates how large organizations use databases and APIs to collect, manage, and share information. By querying real health datasets, students gain experience using data retrieval tools while developing analytical skills and a greater understanding of global health issues.
